# SoftwareX calibration (ISSN 2352-7110)

Last calibrated: 2026-09-03

Use this reference when the target journal is SoftwareX. The journal identity and ISSN are anchored to the ISSN record.[^issn-softwarex] This document translates the global `Overall` rubric into the journal's actual article and reviewer expectations. It is a calibration aid, not a substitute for reviewing the submitted paper.

## Calibration boundary

Only official SoftwareX criteria and comparable SoftwareX articles may set this journal's numeric bands. Do not import another journal's publication floor, cover designation, article sample, or score distribution. Cross-venue material may inform a general question such as how to inspect robustness, but it is not a comparator for `Overall`.

The examples below are evidence-pattern anchors for reviewing a new submission, not official retrospective scores assigned by SoftwareX. Match article type and claim scope before using one. Scores `1-5` retain the global meanings in `../SKILL.md`; limited novelty, missing optional ablations, a modest evaluation, or understandable language errors do not qualify for `1` or `2`.

## Venue-relative score scale with same-journal examples

### `6` — minimum publishable

A submission is around `6` when the software exists and its central use is credibly demonstrated, the architecture and execution path are understandable, reuse metadata are present, and the claims remain narrow enough for the evidence. Broad comparative superiority is not required when it is not claimed.

- **KubeRule boundary example:** the article documents a three-component implementation, six implemented guardrail check types, dashboard behavior, and an exploratory deployment at one medium-sized financial-technology company. The deployment surfaced privileged containers, missing health probes, and vulnerable images. The authors explicitly state that screenshots and the trial do not establish comparative effectiveness and that longitudinal data, baselines, scalability work, and additional sites are still needed (`§§2.1-2.3`, `§§4-5`, Figs. 1-7; PDF pp. 3-8).[^kuberule-article] This is concrete support for a publishable working-prototype claim, not for a claim of superior security outcomes.
- **Elementti boundary example:** the article traces data through a GUI, shared `AppState`, and numerical `ProcessingEngine`; Fig. 3 shows six outputs from one industrial-boiler SEM-EDS case under different ratio-map, masking, and replacement choices, and the export records the selected settings. It also points to two prior research uses while stating that broader application still needs evaluation (`§§2-4`, Figs. 1-3; PDF pp. 3-6).[^elementti-article] A focused tool can therefore meet the floor with one well-explained real use and bounded impact claims even without a broad benchmark.

If a submission makes broader claims than these examples while offering only screenshots or one demonstration, the evidence no longer supports `6` merely because similar artifacts appear in a published paper.

### `7` — solid and representative

A submission is around `7` when it adds a more complete reusable workflow, testing or reproducibility support, and a convincing end-to-end case, while important breadth or comparison evidence remains future work.

- **DoExpert example:** the analytical engine is an importable unit-tested package exercised in continuous integration; the repository supplies the flat-grinding dataset and reproduction instructions; and the paper follows the case from an L8 experimental design through surrogate selection, NSGA-II/III optimization, and six-method MCDM ranking (`§2.1`, `§3`, Figs. 3-6; PDF pp. 2, 5-8).[^doexpert-article] Tables 3-4 make the integration gap visible, but the tool comparison is qualitative, the demonstrated application is one process case, and broader process validation and quantitative benchmarking are explicitly deferred (PDF pp. 8-9). This is stronger than a single functional demonstration but does not by itself establish an `8`.
- **ReAutoPrognostics example:** the workflow evaluates 31 time-domain feature combinations with seven AutoML frameworks on one industrial rolling-mill dataset and the public NASA C-MAPSS FD001 subset, reporting accuracy and training-time trade-offs (`§3`, Figs. 2-4, Tables 2-3; PDF pp. 4-6).[^reautoprognostics-article] The same configuration-driven pipeline runs both cases, but evaluation uses one fixed 80/20 split; the authors explicitly defer cross-validation and rolling-window validation (`§5`, PDF p. 6). Multiple end-to-end cases and traceable configurations support `7`, while the single split leaves robustness short of the `8` pattern.
- **RehabBot example:** PPO, TD3, and SAC train on upper- and lower-limb environments; five-seed convergence and velocity, acceleration, jerk, and torque traces show that the Gymnasium environment is stable and learnable (`§2.2.4`, Figs. 3-6; PDF pp. 5-7).[^rehabbot-article] The authors correctly frame this as software validation rather than an algorithm comparison, and they identify simplified target-reaching tasks, absent musculoskeletal dynamics, no clinically validated protocol, and unresolved simulation-to-real transfer (`§5`, PDF pp. 7-8). This supports a reusable validated simulator without converting simulated safety traces into clinical-efficacy evidence.

### `8` — strong

A submission is around `8` when it goes beyond feature demonstration and tests central claims quantitatively across multiple cases, repetitions, implementations, or failure conditions. At least one material advantage over the `7` pattern must be observable in the results rather than asserted in the impact section.

- **PVMSim example:** GUI and CLI share one execution pipeline; every run exports configuration, logs, summaries, MATLAB results, and SHA-256 checksums; and an automated smoke test checks the benchmark result and required artifacts (`§§2.2-2.3`, Algorithm 1; PDF pp. 3-4).[^pvmsim-article] The evaluation reports 30 runs with seeds 42-71 on three PV modules and compares minimum, mean, maximum, and standard deviation against published methods (Table 1; PDF pp. 4-6). It also reports the unfavorable Photowatt-PWP201 result and explains why differing parameterizations, budgets, and untested bound effects prevent a causal superiority claim (`§§3.1-3.2`, PDF p. 5). Repetition plus an honest negative result is stronger evidence than a polished success case alone.
- **Modular SRF example:** executable examples traverse the same Flask calculation route used by the interface and cover five workflow families. Tables 3-4 test two open solvers plus an external reference, two tolerances, 200/2,000/20,000 samples, five random seeds, three exact repeats, and an infeasible case with a known repair (`§2.5`, PDF pp. 4-5).[^modular-srf-article] The software also exposes eight configurations and versioned exports, but the paper states that exhaustive input coverage, formal continuous integration, concurrent-load benchmarks, and structured usability studies are absent (`§5.1`, PDF p. 8). That combination is a concrete `8` pattern: broad and quantified validation with bounded remaining gaps.
- **TUCAN example:** the released models, bilingual datasets, and evaluator are tested on an 840-case, six-scenario function-calling benchmark. Tables 6-10 report repeated runs, mean and standard deviation, McNemar tests, four general-purpose baselines, per-scenario errors, and Bulgarian-language retention: gains over the BgGPT bases are +22.42 and +15.42 percentage points for the 2.6B and 9B models, while the 27B gain is explicitly reported as non-significant (`§§2.2-4`, PDF pp. 3-5).[^tucan-article] Synthetic training-data homogeneity, limited adversarial cases, and no formal inter-annotator agreement remain visible limitations (PDF p. 3), keeping the result strong rather than conclusive.
- **FastTWD example:** the paper compares the reference implementation with matrix-free CPU and GPU variants on synthetic tensors and four real datasets, then varies tensor order, mode size, and rank in separate scalability sweeps (`§§3.1-3.3`, Tables 3-4, Figs. 3-5; PDF pp. 4-6).[^fasttwd-article] It reports runtime, sampled peak memory, iterations, and reconstruction error, including baseline cases that could not complete at larger orders. Rank-dependent cost and hardware-specific GPU conclusions are stated as limits (`§3.3`, PDF p. 6). This is a concrete `8`: matched quantitative baselines across real and synthetic cases, without claiming hardware-independent superiority.
- **DPArena example:** every form submission executes clean, poisoned, clean-plus-defence, and poisoned-plus-defence scenarios; the examples vary poisoning intensity and compare several aggregation defences, then reproduce one configuration on a separately deployed instance (`§§3-4.4`, Figs. 4-5, Tables 1-3; PDF pp. 4-6).[^dparena-article] Configuration serialization, fixed environments, seed management, and cross-deployment deltas make the reproducibility claim observable. The supported attacks, defences, and two illustrative datasets remain bounded, so the evidence supports a strong platform claim rather than universal defence rankings.

### `9` — exceptional within SoftwareX

A `9` clearly exceeds the `8` controls on several reinforcing axes; it does not merely add one larger table.

- **RALFuzz example:** Table 8 repeats the complete two-stage pipeline three times across parser, buffer, lifecycle, stateful, file-handle, and custom-allocator APIs. Tables 9-10 add signature-only and component ablations; Tables 11-12 add same-API OSS-Fuzz and public LLM4FDG context; and Tables 13-14 execute exported harnesses under both libFuzzer and AFL++ with sanitizer instrumentation (`§4.2`, PDF pp. 9-12).[^ralfuzz-article] The paper distinguishes behaviour diversity from downstream edge coverage, reports targets where gains are small, preserves rejected artifacts, and quantifies token and time costs. Its limits are equally specific: a four-API external subset, 60/300-second fuzzing budgets, no full cited-tool sweep, and no long-running industrial campaign (PDF pp. 10-12). Breadth, ablation, failure-boundary analysis, and restrained claims jointly make this a `9` anchor, while those unresolved scope limits separate it from `10`.
- **JOSSeph example:** continuous integration runs the real extractors, and 40 repository-extractor comparisons on ten pinned repositories match standalone outputs row by row (`§2.3`, PDF p. 3).[^josseph-article] The main run processes 500 repositories into 1,767,564 records; Tables 3-4 report every partial failure and per-extractor coverage, while §3.1 explains all 85 extractor failures rather than discarding them (`§3`, PDF p. 4). The archived dataset retains configurations, resolved commits, and validation results, and the analysis exposes a size-confounded correlation reversal (Table 5). Java/default-branch restriction, time-dependent GitHub observations, and the lack of content-aware cache fingerprints remain explicit (`§5`, PDF p. 5). Scale, exact validation, failure accounting, and reusable artifacts reinforce one another enough for `9`, but those boundaries still matter.

### `10` — top-end SoftwareX exemplar

Use the global `10` definition only when the intrinsic evidence materially exceeds both `9` anchors and leaves no unresolved weakness threatening the central claim. Relative to RALFuzz, that could mean a matched full-suite comparison plus long-running independent deployments rather than short-budget compatibility checks. Relative to JOSSeph, it could mean independently reproduced end-to-end runs across languages and branches with content-addressed provenance rather than artifact-presence caching. These are concrete contrasts with SoftwareX papers, not a universal checklist. Publication or any editorial label remains solely a post-validator and cannot supply the missing evidence.

Do not assign every cached article exactly `6`. Publication establishes the lower bound; the exact score requires the same full evidence pass used for the manuscript under review.

## What SoftwareX evaluates

SoftwareX is part of Elsevier's Research Elements portfolio, and its official review form separates four judgments:[^softwarex-research-elements][^softwarex-reviewer-form]

1. Software impact: enabling new research questions, improving existing research, reuse or uptake, and possible commercial use.
2. Manuscript quality: clear title/abstract/keywords and scientific scope; contribution to discovery; adequate software architecture description; clear experimental setting and evaluation; convincing results; current references; complete metadata tables.
3. Software quality: evidence that it runs, reproducibility, coding standards, open-source licensing, API/user documentation, dependencies and operating environment, installation guidance, and tests.
4. Recommendation and empirical adequacy: accept, revision, resubmission, or reject, together with whether the empirical evaluation is thorough, acceptable, not thorough, or not applicable.

Apply those criteria proportionally to the article type:

- Incremental contribution can still be publishable; the official impact scale explicitly includes a `Fair - incremental contribution` category. Weak novelty alone is not a fatal defect.
- An empirical evaluation marked not applicable for a defensible article type must not be treated as missing evidence.
- Failure to run the software because the reviewer did not test it lowers confidence. It is not evidence that the software fails.
- A missing license, unusable installation path, non-running core artifact, or results contradicted by the supplied evidence can be publication-blocking because these are central SoftwareX criteria.
- Count only flaws that materially affect impact, manuscript quality, software quality, or claim validity when deciding whether a draft falls below `6`.

## Calibration procedure

1. Match the submission to the closest cached SoftwareX article type and technical ambition; do not use an unrelated SoftwareX paper solely because it is newer, and never substitute a paper from another journal.
2. Compare the manuscript on the four official judgment groups above, then apply the global severity definitions.
3. Ask whether each weakness invalidates the central contribution or is a normal revision-level limitation. Only the former can drive a manuscript materially below the published floor.
4. State the specific cached controls used and why they are comparable. If none are comparable, say so and rely on the official criteria plus the global rubric.
5. Assign the provisional score from intrinsic evidence only, then run the applicable status post-validators in `../SKILL.md`. Use only verified SoftwareX metadata for venue-specific validation and resolve any mismatch by evidence-based comparative re-review.
6. Keep `Decision` consistent with the binary output contract: `Overall` 1-5 is `Reject`; 6-10 is `Accept`. Put minor/major revision advice in the prose.

## Refresh rule

Before a new SoftwareX review, check the journal's latest publication metadata. Add newer lawfully public version-of-record PDFs when available, inspect their score-relevant evidence, and update this file's date, concrete anchors, and corpus table. Keep every numeric boundary grounded in SoftwareX; leave a band explicitly under-calibrated rather than filling it with examples from another journal.
